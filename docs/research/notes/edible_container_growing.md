# Home/Container Growing of Peppers, Tomatoes and Spring Onions (Tropical vs Temperate)

Research date: 2026-10-14. Note: several primary pages (UF/IFAS EDIS VH034 container guide, RHS chilli page, CSIR-CRI KAFACI article) returned 410/404 on fetch; some findings below come via search-result summaries of those or related extension pages and are flagged as such. Weight university extension over blogs/seed-retailer content where they conflict.

## 1. Crop stages, timings, container sizes and soil

### Takeaway
Tomatoes and peppers follow a ~6-8 week seedling phase, then roughly 60-80 days (tomato) and 80-100 days (pepper) from transplant to first harvest; peppers need warmer germination (soil min ~60°F/16°C, ideally 80-90°F on a heat mat). Bunching onions/scallions are the fastest (50-65 days from direct seed). Pots: tomato ~5 gal / 30-45 cm; pepper 3-4 gal; minimum ~10 in wide x 10 in deep, with good drainage.

### Cited Findings
**Germination**
- Soil temperature for germination (Harrington table, Oregon State/UC Davis): tomato min 50°F, optimum range 60-85°F; pepper min 60°F, range 65-95°F; onion min 35°F, range 50-95°F — [Oregon State Extension](https://extension.oregonstate.edu/es/gardening/soil-compost/soil-temperature-conditions-vegetable-seed-germination)
- Tomato seed germinates in ~6-8 days within the optimal range; at 50°F it can take over 40 days — [UC ANR chart (oc4h.ucanr.edu)](https://oc4h.ucanr.edu/sites/default/files/2021-01/343966.pdf)
- RHS: tomato seed needs about 18°C (64°F) to germinate; seedlings usually appear within a fortnight; move to maximum light immediately to prevent legginess — [RHS tomatoes](https://www.rhs.org.uk/vegetables/tomatoes/grow-your-own)
- Peppers: keep flats at 80-90°F with a heat mat until emergence; ~70°F soil is ideal; heat mats dry mix quickly — [UMN Extension peppers](https://extension.umn.edu/vegetables/growing-peppers)
- Onion emergence ~4 days at 25°C, 5 days at 20°C, 7 days at 15°C; drops sharply at 35°C (relevant to hot tropical sowing) — [Allotment Garden chart, secondary](https://www.allotment-garden.org/gardening-information/best-temperatures-for-seed-germination/)
- Pepper/tomato seed may take 3+ weeks when soil is too cold — [Creative Vegetable Gardener (blog)](https://www.creativevegetablegardener.com/how-to-germinate-seeds/)

**Seedling to transplant**
- Tomato: prick out into individual pots at a couple of true leaves; keep ≥16°C; ready for final position after about a month; plant out when temps reliably above 16°C; harden off; plant deep so first leaves sit just above soil — [RHS tomatoes](https://www.rhs.org.uk/vegetables/tomatoes/grow-your-own)
- Tomato seed typically started 7-9 weeks before last frost (seed retailer) — via [search summary; Illinois/WSU results](https://pubs.extension.wsu.edu/growing-tomatoes-in-home-gardens-home-garden-series)
- Pepper: start seed ~8 weeks before setting outside; ¼ in deep in sterile soilless mix; set out once night lows stay above 50°F; bring in if nights <55°F — [UMN Extension](https://extension.umn.edu/vegetables/growing-peppers)
- Pepper transplants ideally 5-6 weeks old, 5-6 leaves, 6-8 in tall (university-style source); a Ghana-focused guide says transplant at 4-6 true leaves, 6-8 weeks after sowing — [search summary: Valley View Univ. / Ghana guide](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/opoku-eric-david-sweet-pepper-28560333/28560333) (lower-quality; verify)

**Days to harvest / yield**
- Tomatoes: 65-80 days (WSU general range); average yield 4-10 lb per plant depending on variety (not container-specific) — [WSU Extension](https://pubs.extension.wsu.edu/growing-tomatoes-in-home-gardens-home-garden-series)
- Determinate examples: Bush Early Girl ~65 days from transplant; Containers Choice Red ~70 days from transplant; Tiny Tim cherry ~60 days — [WSU](https://pubs.extension.wsu.edu/growing-tomatoes-in-home-gardens-home-garden-series); [True Leaf Market](https://www.trueleafmarket.com/a/amp/products/tomato-seeds-containers-choice-red-f1)
- Determinate types flower in a flush and ripen over a few weeks (single concentrated harvest) — [Epic Gardening](https://www.epicgardening.com/determinate-tomato-varieties/)
- Pepper harvest starts 80-100 days from transplanting, or 3-6 weeks after flowering — [search summary, Ghana-focused guide](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/stev-project/28560362) (lower-quality)
- Peppers can be picked green at mature size; plants keep flowering/fruiting as you harvest — [UMN](https://extension.umn.edu/vegetables/growing-peppers)
- Bunching onions (Johnny's): Guardsman 50 days, Deep Purple 60, Parade 65, Evergreen Hardy White 65 days from direct seed; subtract 10-15 days when counting from transplant; pH 6.2-6.8; sow ¼ in apart in 2-3 in bands, ¼-½ in deep; or 6-8 seeds per cell in 72-cell trays, transplant clusters 6-8 in apart — [Johnny's Parade](https://www.johnnyseeds.com/vegetables/onions/bunching-onions/parade-organic-onion-seed-3850G.html); [Johnny's Guardsman](https://www.johnnyseeds.com/vegetables/onions/bunching-onions/guardsman-onion-seed-29.11.html)

**Containers and soil**
- Tomato: 30-45 cm (12-18 in) pot for one plant, or growing bag for two — [RHS](https://www.rhs.org.uk/vegetables/tomatoes/grow-your-own)
- Tomato: 5-gallon container with plenty of drainage (Palm Beach County Extension, FL); 'Micro-Tom' dwarf fits 4-inch pots — [PBC Extension PDF](https://discover.pbc.gov/coextension/ArticleArchives/Tomatogardening.pdf)
- Peppers/eggplant: 3-4 gal may suffice; tomatoes 5 gal or larger; 4-5 drainage holes ~½ in diameter — [UF/IFAS Lafayette Co. blog](https://blogs.ifas.ufl.edu/lafayetteco/2020/04/14/container-gardening-with-kids/)
- Minimum container 10 in wide x 10 in deep — [UF/IFAS Leon Co. blog](https://blogs.ifas.ufl.edu/leonco/2016/04/27/dont-think-you-have-a-green-thumb-try-container-gardening)
- Pepper soil pH 6.5-7 — [UMN](https://extension.umn.edu/vegetables/growing-peppers); container media pH ~6.0 and EC ~2.0-2.5 mmho/cm (Cornell) — [Cornell Veg Edge](https://rvpadmin.cce.cornell.edu/pdf/veg_edge/pdf241_pdf.pdf)
- Use sterile soilless/peat-free mix for seed starting — [UMN](https://extension.umn.edu/vegetables/growing-peppers); [RHS](https://www.rhs.org.uk/vegetables/tomatoes/grow-your-own)

### Inferences
- An app timeline could use defaults: Tomato — germination 5-14 d, transplant ~4-8 wk after sowing, first flower roughly 3-5 wk after transplant (not directly cited), first ripe fruit 60-80 d post-transplant. Pepper — germination 7-21 d, transplant 6-8 wk, harvest 80-100 d post-transplant (green earlier, colored later). Scallion — 50-65 d from seed.
- In Accra, ambient temps (~24-32°C) sit within optimal germination ranges year-round, so heat mats are unnecessary; the onion germination drop at 35°C suggests shading seed trays in the hottest months.

### Gaps
- No authoritative container-specific yield per plant for tomato or pepper found; scallion yield per pot not found.
- UF/IFAS VH034 container size/plants-per-pot table could not be fetched (410).
- Days to first flower not cited directly for either crop.
- No authoritative pepper days-to-germination figure (only blog "up to 3 weeks when cold").

## 2. Watering and fertiliser by stage; blossom-end rot; over/under-fertilising signs

### Takeaway
Keep moisture even (containers may need daily, even twice-daily watering in heat); avoid excess nitrogen (leafy, few flowers); switch to high-potassium feeds once fruits swell (RHS: every 10-14 days). Blossom-end rot is mostly a water-delivery/calcium-transport problem, not low soil calcium.

### Cited Findings
- Tomato: once first fruits start to swell, feed container plants every 10-14 days with high-potassium liquid fertiliser — [RHS](https://www.rhs.org.uk/vegetables/tomatoes/grow-your-own)
- Keep compost evenly moist; uneven moisture causes fruit splitting and blossom-end rot; containers may need daily watering in hot weather; curling leaves can signal shortage — [RHS](https://www.rhs.org.uk/vegetables/tomatoes/grow-your-own)
- Too much nitrogen produces bushy, leafy peppers slow to bear fruit; apply P and K per soil test — [UMN](https://extension.umn.edu/vegetables/growing-peppers)
- Excessive N fertilisation is a listed cause of poor blossom/fruit set in tomato and pepper — [UMD Extension](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/poor-blossom-and-fruit-set-vegetables)
- Avoid overhead watering (wet leaves encourage disease); keep soil moisture consistent — [UMN](https://extension.umn.edu/vegetables/growing-peppers)
- Potassium deficiency: marginal browning/scorch starting on older leaves, leaf cupping/rolling; K demand rises as plants shift to fruiting — [UC ANR nutrient presentation 2026](https://www.ucanr.edu/sites/default/files/2026-02/How%20to%20Recognize%20Visual%20Signs%20of%20Nutrient%20Issues%20in%20Vegetable%20Crops%20Presentation%202.23.2026.pdf); [Epic Gardening](https://www.epicgardening.com/tomato-nutrient-deficiency/)
- Leaf-edge browning may also indicate over-fertilisation (salts) — via search summary; UMN suggests foliar lab test to confirm — [UMN IPM blog](https://blog-fruit-vegetable-ipm.extension.umn.edu/2025/08/weekly-vegetable-update-august-6-2025.html)
- Blossom-end rot: calcium deficiency in fruit caused by inconsistent watering; container plants that dry out can't move calcium to fruit ends; if you can't water daily (or twice daily) use a self-watering/reservoir container — [UW-Madison Extension Marinette](https://marinette.extension.wisc.edu/horticulture/container-gardening/tomato-in-a-container_sr); [Ask Extension](https://ask2.extension.org/kb/faq.php?id=766775)
- Once BER appears there is no fix for that fruit; correcting watering protects later fruit — [NMSU](https://swyg.nmsu.edu/2000/051300.html)
- Water until water exits drainage holes — [Morton Arboretum](https://mortonarb.org/plant-and-protect/tree-plant-care/plant-care-resources/blossom-end-rot/); RHS BER page — [RHS](https://www.rhs.org.uk/problems/blossom-end-rot)
- Peppers also suffer BER and sunscald — [UMN](https://extension.umn.edu/vegetables/growing-peppers)

### Inferences
- Stage-based feeding prompt: seedling (dilute balanced feed once true leaves appear — not directly cited), vegetative (balanced, moderate N), flowering/fruiting (switch to high-K every 10-14 days). Scallions are leafy so a balanced/N-leaning feed fits (inferred).
- Care prompt triggers: "lush leaves, no flowers" → cut N; "brown leaf edges on older leaves" → possible K shortage or salt build-up (flush pot).

### Gaps
- No extension source gave specific NPK ratios or ml/L for containers; scallion feeding guidance not found.

## 3. Pollination indoors/balconies, flower drop, fruit set in heat

### Takeaway
Tomatoes and peppers are self-fertile but need movement; tap/shake flowers or buzz with an electric toothbrush. Flower drop is mostly temperature-driven: tomato set falls off below ~55°F (13°C) nights or above ~90-95°F (32-35°C) days, and with warm nights >75°F (24°C). Peppers drop flowers at nights <60°F or >75°F or days >95°F. In Accra, warm nights are the key limit; choose heat-set/cherry varieties and give afternoon shade.

### Cited Findings
- Tomato flower/fruit production diminishes below 55°F or above 95°F; cold springs cause catfacing of the first truss — [UMD Extension](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/poor-blossom-and-fruit-set-vegetables)
- Pepper flowers/small fruit drop when nights <60°F or >75°F, or days >95°F; plants resume setting in late summer/fall — [UMD](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/poor-blossom-and-fruit-set-vegetables)
- Other causes: excessive shade, excess N, prolonged dry or wet soil, hot dry winds, not harvesting regularly — [UMD](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/poor-blossom-and-fruit-set-vegetables)
- Mitigation: wet foliage during hot dry spells; site for late afternoon shade — [UMD](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/poor-blossom-and-fruit-set-vegetables)
- Tomato thresholds conflict: OSU — nights <~60°F or days >~92°F; NC State — nights <55°F or >75°F; Arizona — days >95°F; a Minnesota-linked EDIS doc — 85°F. Pollen becomes non-viable outside range — [OSU](https://extension.okstate.edu/programs/gardening/grow-gardening-columns/grow-columns-2021/june-20-2021-tomato-blossom-drop.html); [NC State Burke Co.](https://burke.ces.ncsu.edu/blossom-drop); [U Arizona PDF](https://extension.arizona.edu/sites/extension.arizona.edu/files/attachment/flowerandfruitdrop_0.pdf)
- Cherry tomatoes set over a wider temperature range than large-fruited types — [U Arizona](https://extension.arizona.edu/sites/extension.arizona.edu/files/attachment/flowerandfruitdrop_0.pdf)
- Hormone blossom-set sprays help with cold-night drop but little in high heat — [OSU](https://extension.okstate.edu/programs/gardening/grow-gardening-columns/grow-columns-2021/june-20-2021-tomato-blossom-drop.html)
- Pepper growth weakened with days >90°F or nights <60°F or >70°F — [UMN](https://extension.umn.edu/vegetables/growing-peppers)
- Ghana: CSIR researcher warned tomato crops can fail when temps exceed 32°C; CSIR-CRI with Korea (RDA/KAFACI) running a 5-year heat-tolerant tomato breeding project — [GNA, Aug 2026](https://gna.org.gh/2026/08/ghanas-tomato-ginger-shortfalls-deepen-import-bill-csir-researchers-say/); [CSIR news](https://csir.org.gh/multimedia/news/csir-korea-team-up-to-develop-heat-tolerant-tomatoes)
- Pollination: RHS — lightly tap or shake open flowers; misting flowers helps; open greenhouse vents for insects — [RHS](https://www.rhs.org.uk/vegetables/tomatoes/grow-your-own)
- Tomatoes are buzz-pollinated (bumblebees, not honeybees); Montana State extension lesson uses electric toothbrush on flower clusters — [MSU Extension PDF](https://www.montana.edu/extension/bigskybees/documents/4B%20a%20%20Buzz-pollinationLessonPlan%20v%2010-17.pdf)
- Toothbrush technique: touch to the flower cluster stem/truss, not inside the flower, every 2-3 days while flowers open, around midday — [Gardener's Path](https://gardenerspath.com/plants/vegetables/hand-pollinate-tomatoes/) (blog)
- One EDIS-hosted doc says tomato flowers must be pollinated within ~50 hours or abort — [EDIS doc](https://ojs.test.flvc.org/edis/article/download/119975/118073/177961)

### Inferences
- App can use local weather forecasts: flag "flower drop risk" when night lows >24°C or day highs >32-35°C (tomato) — in Accra this is frequent in Feb-April (hottest months; not cited here).
- Prompt: "shake/buzz flowers every 2-3 days at midday" when first flower logged and plant is on a sheltered balcony/indoors.

### Gaps
- Pepper hand pollination guidance (cotton swab/brush) not found in an authoritative source.
- No authoritative list of heat-set tomato varieties available in Ghana (CRI-Kwabena Kwabena and CRI-Copia are released OPVs but not described as heat-tolerant).

## 4. Spring onions: scraps vs seed, cut-and-come-again, regrowth cycles

### Takeaway
Scraps (white base with roots) re-sprout within about a week and give typically 2-5 regrowths before flavour/vigour decline — fewer in water than soil. Seed-grown bunching onions take 50-65 days and give stronger, longer-lived plants. No extension source gives an official cycle count.

### Cited Findings
- UC Master Gardener (UC ANR) blog: scraps rooted in pots sprout within about a week; no cycle count given — [UC ANR](https://ucanr.edu/node/152510)
- 2-4 regrowths for healthy bases, ~1 if root nodules darkened — [Epic Gardening](https://www.epicgardening.com/regrow-green-onions/)
- 3-5 regrowths typical; water-grown loses flavour each cycle, soil retains it — [BHG via AOL](https://www.aol.com/articles/regrow-green-onions-kitchen-scraps-175929282.html); [Kitchprep](https://kitchprep.com/how-many-times-can-you-regrow-green-onions/) (low-authority)
- Green onions give ~4-8 weeks continuous yield before plant tires (citing UMN/USDA NIFA kitchen garden guidance) — [Farmers' Almanac](https://www.farmersalmanac.com/regrow-vegetables-from-kitchen-scraps)
- Seed: 50-65 days direct-seeded (varieties above); sow early spring for summer, July/Aug for fall/spring (temperate) — [Johnny's](https://www.johnnyseeds.com/vegetables/onions/bunching-onions/parade-organic-onion-seed-3850G.html)

### Inferences
- App can track "regrowth cycle #" and prompt to move water-rooted scraps into soil after roots form, and to replace after ~3-5 cuts. Cutting above the white base (leave ~2-3 cm) is common practice but not authoritatively cited here.
- In Accra scallions can be grown year-round in containers (cool-season crop but non-bulbing; tolerates heat with water). This is an inference; not cited.

### Gaps
- No extension-backed number for regrowth cycles; no data on harvest interval between cuts (commonly 1-2 weeks, uncited).

## 5. Pests and diseases in containers; tropical issues

### Takeaway
Core container pests: aphids, whitefly (and the viruses it spreads), spider mites; diseases: damping-off in seedlings, early/late blight, grey mould, bacterial spot. In Ghana, whitefly-transmitted tomato leaf curl virus, bacterial wilt and root-knot nematodes are major, and hot-wet season flooding/waterlogging and disease sharply cut yields.

### Cited Findings
- Tomato problems: blight (warm wet weather, outdoors), fruit splitting from fluctuating moisture, grey mould, glasshouse whitefly and sap suckers (honeydew) — [RHS](https://www.rhs.org.uk/vegetables/tomatoes/grow-your-own)
- Pepper pests: cutworms, aphids, hornworms; diseases: bacterial spot and viruses (remove infected plants; incurable) — [UMN](https://extension.umn.edu/vegetables/growing-peppers)
- Damping-off: prevent with sterile well-drained mix, clean containers (dilute bleach), avoid overwatering/overcrowding, let surface dry somewhat after germination, clean water; discard infected seedlings with soil — [Iowa State](https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/faq/some-seedlings-i-start-indoors-collapse-and-die-shortly-after-germination-why); [Utah State](https://extension.usu.edu/pests/research/damping-off.php); [Penn State](https://extension.psu.edu/safeguard-your-seedlings-from-damping-off)
- Ghana: whitefly-transmitted tomato leaf curl (geminivirus) is widespread with severe yield losses; spraying is ineffective against the virus; host-free periods reduce whitefly; resistant varieties recommended — [UG Space review](https://ugspace.ug.edu.gh/bitstreams/445f6523-033d-4037-b18d-889904b84cfa/download); [AGRIS](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/647225fa2a40512c710f2f47)
- Ghana tomato pests list includes Bemisia tabaci, leaf curl viruses, root-knot nematodes — [Pestinfo](https://www.pestinfo.org/ISPIdata/public/countries/L115-52b)
- Tomato insects most abundant in the Ghanaian dry season (1996 survey) — via [UG Space](https://ugspace.ug.edu.gh/server/api/core/bitstreams/aad27987-3e35-411e-bcc1-681352ffa97f/content)
- Ghana constraints: bacterial wilt, nematodes, acidic soils, low organic matter, recycled hybrid seed; CRI-Kwabena Kwabena showed 48% bacterial wilt incidence in trials — [GNA 2026](https://gna.org.gh/2026/08/ghanas-tomato-ginger-shortfalls-deepen-import-bill-csir-researchers-say/); [CSIR-CRI](https://cropsresearch.org/?p=4076)
- Hot-wet season tropics: flooding, waterlogged soils, diseases and heat significantly reduce tomato yields; grafting onto eggplant rootstock gave 20-100% higher yield and 90-100% flood survival; rain shelters help but mixed results — [WorldVeg PDF](https://worldveg.tind.io/record/39571/files/e03430.pdf); [AVRDC](https://avrdc.org/?p=11329)

### Inferences
- For container growers, waterlogging risk in heavy Accra rains (May-June) is managed by drainage holes, raised pots, and moving pots under cover; container culture with fresh potting mix also sidesteps soil-borne bacterial wilt and nematodes (inference).
- Spider mites thrive in hot, dry conditions (dry season/Harmattan) — general knowledge, not cited here.

### Gaps
- No authoritative source found on spider mites or early blight specifically in containers; no fruit fly/pepper-specific pest data for Accra.

## 6. Seasons and planting calendars: tropical West Africa vs temperate

### Takeaway
Temperate: sow tomatoes/peppers indoors late winter (6-8 weeks before last frost), plant out when nights >10-16°C, harvest mid-summer to frost. Accra (coastal savanna): bimodal rains — major season ~March/April-June, minor ~Sept-Oct, 600-1,000 mm/yr; vegetables are grown both rain-fed and via dry-season irrigation, and container growers with water can grow all three crops year-round, with tomato being hardest in the hottest/wettest periods.

### Cited Findings
- Coastal Savannah zone: major rainy season March/April-June, minor Sept-Oct, average rainfall 600-1,000 mm — [FAO Ghana country report](https://www.fao.org/fileadmin/templates/agphome/documents/PGR/SoW1/africa/GHANA.PDF)
- Accra: ~47 ha rain-fed vegetables, plus ~100 ha irrigated vegetables in the dry season — [RUAF/IWMI Accra background](https://ruaf.iwmi.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/8/2017/02/Accra-Background-Info.pdf)
- Over 60% of Ghana's fresh tomato imports arrive Nov-April (dry season shortfall) — [GNA 2026](https://gna.org.gh/2026/08/ghanas-tomato-ginger-shortfalls-deepen-import-bill-csir-researchers-say/)
- Tomato is Ghana's major vegetable; pepper, eggplant, okra follow — [IntechOpen chapter](https://api.intechopen.com/chapter/pdf-download/80203.pdf)
- 80-90% of onions in Ghanaian markets imported (uncertain estimate) — [MoFA-IFPRI brief](https://policyvault.africa/wp-content/uploads/policy/GHA64.pdf)
- Temperate (UK): sow tomatoes late Feb-mid March (greenhouse) or late March-early April (outdoor); plant out early summer when >16°C; ripening from mid-summer — [RHS](https://www.rhs.org.uk/vegetables/tomatoes/grow-your-own)
- Temperate (Minnesota): peppers out when nights stay >50°F — [UMN](https://extension.umn.edu/vegetables/growing-peppers)
- ASNAPP-Ghana bird's eye chili poster includes a Ghana growing calendar (archived) — [UC Davis Horticulture](https://blog.horticulture.ucdavis.edu/information/birds-eye-chili-best-practices)

### Inferences
- Accra container calendar (inferred): peppers and scallions year-round; tomato best sown so fruiting falls in cooler, drier spells (e.g. nursery in late Oct-Nov after minor rains so fruiting is Jan-Feb; or mid-year July-Aug "little dry season", Accra's coolest period). Avoid fruit set during peak heat (Feb-April) and heavy May-June rains, or use shade/cover. Needs verification against MoFA/GMet data.
- Accra container growers depend on irrigation during the Nov-March dry season and Harmattan (dry winds increase water needs).

### Gaps
- No MoFA/CSIR month-by-month planting calendar for tomato, pepper or onion in the Accra coastal savanna was found online.
- The July-August "little dry season" and Harmattan timing are not cited in sources gathered here.

## 7. Stage-based events to log

### Takeaway
Sources consistently anchor timing on sow date, emergence, true leaves (pricking out), transplant (days-to-maturity often counted from here), first flower/truss, first fruit swelling (feeding switch), and harvest; app logs should map to these.

### Cited Findings
- Days to maturity for tomatoes/bunching onions are often counted from transplant, not sowing (Johnny's: subtract 10-15 days from DTM when transplanting) — [Johnny's](https://www.johnnyseeds.com/vegetables/onions/bunching-onions/parade-organic-onion-seed-3850G.html); [WSU](https://pubs.extension.wsu.edu/growing-tomatoes-in-home-gardens-home-garden-series)
- Key actions tied to stages: pricking out at "couple of true leaves"; feeding switch "once first fruits start to swell"; harvest when fully coloured — [RHS](https://www.rhs.org.uk/vegetables/tomatoes/grow-your-own)
- Pepper harvest 3-6 weeks after flowering — [Ghana-focused guide (lower quality)](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/stev-project/28560362)
- Pollinate within ~50 h of flower opening — [EDIS doc](https://ojs.test.flvc.org/edis/article/download/119975/118073/177961)

### Inferences
- Recommended log events: sow date; germination/emergence (count); first true leaves; pot-up/prick-out; hardening-off start (temperate only); transplant (DTM clock start); first flower/truss; pollination actions; first fruit set; first fruit swelling (→ switch to high-K); first ripe/harvest; harvest weight/count per pick; problems observed (BER, flower drop, pests) with photo; for scallions, cut date and regrowth cycle number.
- Weather-linked events (night temp >24°C, day >32°C, heavy rain) can explain flower drop/BER in logs.

### Gaps
- No source found describing which events gardeners themselves most value logging; the list above is derived from the stage milestones in extension guidance.
