# Rootline wins by rewarding restraint and lineage

Rootline should be a **care-first, check-based plant companion whose differentiators are a propagation family tree and a forgiving emotional layer, not another photo-ID subscription**. The science points one way: extension services say to water when the soil says so, not by the calendar, and overwatering is the leading killer. Yet the market leaders charge roughly **$30–48 a year**, and users mostly complain about paywalls, confidently wrong IDs and rigid schedules that overwater. Gamification research adds a warning. A naive "watered today" streak would reward exactly the behaviour that kills houseplants. Rootline should reward the *soil check* and the *correct skip*, celebrate intact progress, and let plant faces invite rather than accuse. The open gap is an app that serves the collector who also grows edibles: lineage plus provenance, harvest logging for peppers, tomatoes and spring onions, and seasons that follow location (an Accra harmattan as well as a heated temperate winter). Mainstream apps don't model lineage. A cluster of pre-scale indie apps does, so the window is real but narrow, and Planta has already added "seed and propagation tracking". The data stack can cost about **€0/month at 1k MAU and roughly €150–300/month at 10k MAU** if it pairs Pl@ntNet ID with the CC0 World Flora Online backbone and a self-curated care table. The biggest non-product risks are the name, which sits one letter from the existing iOS app "Rootling: Plant Propagation", ASPCA toxicity content with no reuse licence found, and plant patents that restrict propagating some cultivars.

## The science says check the soil, follow the location, and stage the edibles

### Overwatering is the dominant failure, and wilting is ambiguous

Every extension source in the notes ranks improper watering, especially excess, as the top cause of houseplant decline. UConn calls inconsistent watering "perhaps the number one cause of a houseplant's demise." Waterlogged mix drives out oxygen and rots roots, and a root-damaged plant **wilts even in saturated soil** ([UConn](https://homegarden.cahnr.uconn.edu/factsheets/ailing-houseplants/)). Illinois Extension notes that wilting can mean either too little or too much water ([Illinois Extension](https://extension.illinois.edu/houseplants/watering)), and Maryland names overwatering as the most common winter problem ([UMD](https://extension.umd.edu/resources/yard-garden/indoor-plants/indoor-plant-insects)). Fertiliser burn produces blackened leaf edges that resemble overwatering, so any diagnosis flow has to ask about recent feeding. One caution for marketing copy: **no survey or extension dataset in the notes puts a percentage on causes of houseplant death**. The only figure found, "90% of fatalities are preventable," comes from an unsourced commercial blog ([Fantastic Services](https://blog.fantasticservices.com/why-houseplants-die/)). Rootline should not print "X% of plants die from overwatering."

The practical rule is consistent across sources. Illinois says to test with a finger "to a depth of two inches" and to "water your plants when they need it and not according to the calendar." It also warns that probe meters "may not always be reliable" ([Illinois Extension](https://extension.illinois.edu/houseplants/watering)). Cheap analog meters read conductivity, so fertiliser salts and hard water distort them ([Laidback Gardener](https://laidbackgardener.blog/2019/12/19/when-moisture-meters-fail/)). Pot weighing is the best-validated method in nursery research. Oregon calls it "a very effective method" ([Oregon Dept of Agriculture](https://www.oregon.gov/oda/Documents/Publications/NurseryChristmasTree/NRG_20_10.pdf)). Weight-triggered irrigation cut the leaching fraction from 0.14 to 0.06 in one study ([J. Environmental Horticulture](https://jeh.kglmeridian.com/view/journals/jenh/28/2/article-p117.xml)). Weights must be recalibrated as plants grow ([US Patent 10188051](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10188051)). Three caveats apply. No study in the notes compares finger test, meter and pot weight on houseplants specifically. None tests the $10 probe meters. And **no outcome study compares schedule-based with check-based watering for home users**, so the case for "check, don't schedule" rests on expert consensus, not trials.

Drying speed depends on plant type and size, light, temperature, humidity, pot size, mix, drainage and pot material. Unglazed clay dries faster than plastic ([Illinois Extension](https://extension.illinois.edu/houseplants/watering); [NParks](https://nparks.gov.sg/nparksbuzz/issue-03-vol-3-2009/gardening/watering-your-plants-wisely)). Greg says its engine is based on the UN FAO crop-evapotranspiration method ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/05/27/greg-an-app-for-plant-lovers-grows-5-4-million-in-seed-funding)), and its team has said it "errs on the side of less water". That second claim came via a search snippet of an older forum reply ([Greg forum](https://greg.app/question/2qy114/determining-the-amount-of-water-used-for-watering-the-plants-does-anyone-know-how-greg-computes-for)). **Neither Greg's nor Planta's coefficients are published**, and no study in the notes quantifies terracotta-versus-plastic drying or how much a heated winter lengthens intervals. Any multipliers Rootline uses must be calibrated from its own check-in data.

### Seasons are a function of place, not of the calendar

Temperate heated homes in winter combine low light, cooler temperatures and very dry air. Indoor humidity can fall to **10–20% RH** against a preferred 40–60% ([NDSU](https://www.ag.ndsu.edu/news/columns/dakota-gardener/dakota-gardener-winter-houseplant-care); [Nebraska Extension](https://extensionpubs.unl.edu/publication/g2205/pdf/view)). The RHS advises cutting water as days shorten and keeping cacti nearly dry through a winter rest ([RHS](https://www.rhs.org.uk/garden-inspiration/houseplants/winter-interest-houseplants-care)), and UConn advises reducing or pausing feeding during rest periods ([UConn](https://homegarden.cahnr.uconn.edu/factsheets/ailing-houseplants/)). Accra sits near 5°N and has almost no day-length signal. Its cues are the **harmattan**, roughly late November to mid-March, when dry winds make pots dry faster, and the bimodal rains. The coastal-savanna major season runs about March/April–June and the minor one about September–October ([FAO Ghana](https://www.fao.org/fileadmin/templates/agphome/documents/PGR/SoW1/africa/GHANA.PDF)). The harmattan advice comes from a Nigerian blog ([Ounje Aladun](https://ounjealadun.com/portfolio/harmattan-plant-care-tips/)). Its claim that humidity drops "below 5% RH" is **unverified**, and the notes found **no Ghanaian extension research on indoor plant care** at all. Rootline's tropical logic will therefore start as informed inference and needs local validation.

Pests follow the same climate logic. Fungus gnats breed in persistently wet, organic mix, about a 17-day generation at 75°F, and the fixes are drying the surface and repeating Bti about every 5 days ([UC IPM](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/PMG/PESTNOTES/pn7448.html)). Spider mites thrive in hot, dry air and need soap or oil every 5–10 days for a month or more ([Missouri Extension](https://extension.missouri.edu/g7273); [Costa Farms](https://help.costafarms.com/en/knowledge/how-to-get-rid-of-spider-mites)). Soft scale can take 2–4 months ([Arizona Extension](https://extension.arizona.edu/sites/extension.arizona.edu/files/attachment/HouseplantInsect-Reference.pdf)). These are multi-week treatment schedules, which are exactly what a reminder engine does well. A gnat report should also lengthen the watering interval. Spider-mite alerts should rise in heated winters and during the harmattan.

### Edibles need stage clocks and weather triggers, not watering intervals

The persona's peppers, tomatoes and spring onions work differently from houseplants. They follow a stage clock anchored on **transplant date**, which is where days-to-maturity usually starts ([Johnny's](https://www.johnnyseeds.com/vegetables/onions/bunching-onions/parade-organic-onion-seed-3850G.html)). Tomatoes take about **65–80 days** from transplant to harvest ([WSU](https://pubs.extension.wsu.edu/growing-tomatoes-in-home-gardens-home-garden-series)). Peppers need warmer germination (minimum 60°F soil, 80–90°F on a heat mat), are transplanted at about 8 weeks ([UMN](https://extension.umn.edu/vegetables/growing-peppers); [Oregon State](https://extension.oregonstate.edu/es/gardening/soil-compost/soil-temperature-conditions-vegetable-seed-germination)), and reach harvest about 80–100 days after transplant. That last figure comes from a lower-quality Ghana guide ([slideshare](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/stev-project/28560362)). Bunching onions take **50–65 days** from direct seed ([Johnny's](https://www.johnnyseeds.com/vegetables/onions/bunching-onions/guardsman-onion-seed-29.11.html)). Container minimums are about 5 gallons or a 30–45 cm pot for a tomato and 3–4 gallons for a pepper ([RHS](https://www.rhs.org.uk/vegetables/tomatoes/grow-your-own); [UF/IFAS](https://blogs.ifas.ufl.edu/lafayetteco/2020/04/14/container-gardening-with-kids/)).

Watering advice for edibles flips. Containers may need **daily or even twice-daily** watering in heat. Uneven moisture causes fruit splitting and **blossom-end rot**, which is a calcium-transport failure from inconsistent watering, not a lack of calcium in the soil ([RHS](https://www.rhs.org.uk/vegetables/tomatoes/grow-your-own); [UW–Madison](https://marinette.extension.wisc.edu/horticulture/container-gardening/tomato-in-a-container_sr)). Feeding switches to high-potassium every 10–14 days once fruit swells ([RHS](https://www.rhs.org.uk/vegetables/tomatoes/grow-your-own)). Excess nitrogen gives leafy plants with little fruit ([UMD](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/poor-blossom-and-fruit-set-vegetables)).

Heat matters more than anything for an Accra grower. Pepper flowers drop when nights fall below 60°F or rise above 75°F, or when days pass 95°F ([UMD](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/poor-blossom-and-fruit-set-vegetables)). Tomato thresholds conflict between extension services, with night limits from 55–75°F and day limits from 85–95°F ([OSU](https://extension.okstate.edu/programs/gardening/grow-gardening-columns/grow-columns-2021/june-20-2021-tomato-blossom-drop.html); [NC State](https://burke.ces.ncsu.edu/blossom-drop)). A CSIR researcher warned that Ghanaian tomato crops can fail above 32°C ([GNA, Aug 2026](https://gna.org.gh/2026/08/ghanas-tomato-ginger-shortfalls-deepen-import-bill-csir-researchers-say/)). Cherry types set fruit across a wider temperature range ([U Arizona](https://extension.arizona.edu/sites/extension.arizona.edu/files/attachment/flowerandfruitdrop_0.pdf)). Indoors or on a balcony, tomatoes need help with pollination: tap the trusses, or buzz them with an electric toothbrush ([RHS](https://www.rhs.org.uk/vegetables/tomatoes/grow-your-own); [Montana State](https://www.montana.edu/extension/bigskybees/documents/4B%20a%20%20Buzz-pollinationLessonPlan%20v%2010-17.pdf)). Whitefly-borne tomato leaf curl virus is a major threat in Ghana and cannot be sprayed away ([UG Space](https://ugspace.ug.edu.gh/bitstreams/445f6523-033d-4037-b18d-889904b84cfa/download)). Spring-onion scraps re-sprout within about a week ([UC ANR](https://ucanr.edu/node/152510)) and give roughly 2–5 regrowths, but those counts come from blogs and **no extension source sets a cycle count** ([Epic Gardening](https://www.epicgardening.com/regrow-green-onions/)). The notes also found **no MoFA/CSIR month-by-month planting calendar for Accra**, so any Accra sowing calendar Rootline ships is inference.

Propagation evidence is thinner still. The one solid extension timeline is snake-plant leaf cuttings: 4–6 weeks at 70–75°F, best in summer, and **variegated forms may not come true** ([UF/IFAS](https://hort.ifas.ufl.edu/database/lppi/sp349.shtml)). Pothos in water takes roughly 3–6 weeks, and the common advice is to pot up at 1–2 inches of root to limit transplant shock, though both figures come from blogs ([Lively Root](https://livelyroot.com/blogs/plant-care/how-to-propagate-pothos); [Folia Collective](https://foliacollective.com/blogs/news/ask-a-plant-pro-transitioning-propagations-from-water/)). **No sourced success rates exist for any propagation method.** That makes Rootline's own lineage data potentially the best dataset in the hobby, which is a long-term asset for a data-analyst founder.

## The market charges $30–48 a year for reminders and ID, while lineage and harvests sit in indie corners

The houseplant category is mature at the top. **Planta** has 4.8★ from about 114K US ratings and premium tiers up to $47.99/yr ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/planta-keep-your-plants-alive/id1410126781)). **PictureThis** has 4.8★ from about 1.1M ratings, Pro up to $39.99 and a $49.99 family plan ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/picturethis-plant-identifier/id1252497129)). **Greg** has 4.6★ from 21K ratings, $29.99/yr, and reminders behind the paywall ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1512912236)). **PlantIn** reports 35M downloads ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlantIn)). **Pl@ntNet** is free and non-commercial ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pl@ntNet)). ID is effectively commoditised, so paid value has moved to adaptive reminders, diagnosis and light meters. **Vera by Bloomscape has shut down** ([Growli](https://www.getgrowli.app/blog/plant-care-app)), a reminder that free, retailer-funded apps do not last. Market-size reports range from $0.5B to $1.8B for 2025 and use opaque methods ([Growth Market Reports](https://growthmarketreports.com/report/houseplant-care-ai-app-market); [Market Research Store](https://www.marketresearchstore.com/market-insights/plant-identification-apps-market-829263)). They should not appear in any pitch without caveats.

Complaints cluster in three places. The first is **subscription friction**: PictureThis trials that auto-convert, with reviewers "charged $29.99 three days later" ([Botanicaly](https://www.botanicaly.com/best-plant-care-apps); [ComplaintsBoard](https://www.complaintsboard.com/picturethis-plant-identifier-b149857)), and Greg reminders paywalled "prematurely" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1512912236)). The second is **schedules that don't adapt**. Planta reviewers report reminders that ignore repeated snoozes. An AI-generated aggregator claims about 20% of Planta feedback mentions non-adjusting reminders, which is unverified ([Clarity](https://www.onclarity.com/leaderboard/insight/getplanta)). A desert-climate Greg user said following the schedule would have killed their plants ([JustUseApp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1512912236/greg-plant-care-assistant/reviews)). The third is **rigid data models**. Planta can't change a plant's species without deleting its history ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/planta-keep-your-plants-alive/id1410126781)), Greg users can't edit past watering dates, and a Notion-template user can't track multiples individually ([Notion](https://www.notion.com/templates/the-ultimate-plant-care-os)). These are collector pains. Two cautions apply. **Reddit sentiment could not be retrieved**, and no source documented data-export or lock-in complaints, so "no export" is an assumption rather than a sourced pain point.

What users love is just as telling. They praise schedules that account for pot, light and soil, progress photos that build confidence, and community ([App Store: Planta](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/planta-keep-your-plants-alive/id1410126781); [App Store: Greg](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1512912236)). Accumulated history looks like the sticky asset. That makes **portability a credible trust differentiator**, because Rootline can offer it precisely because history is what locks users in elsewhere.

Lineage is the crowded-but-leaderless niche. Planta added "seed and propagation tracking" in v3.108.0 with no parent-child detail described ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/planta-keep-your-plants-alive/id1410126781)). The indies are InPlantory (beta, mother-plant links, aimed at rare aroids) ([InPlantory](https://beta.inplantory.com/)), PropagatePro (seller ROI and QR verification) ([PropagatePro](https://propagatepro.vercel.app/)), Spadix (hybridiser breeding) ([Spadix](https://spadix.app/)), Cutting Log (open source, local-first, CSV export) ([GitHub](https://github.com/rwrife/cutting-log)), Folaria ([Folaria](https://folaria.app/en/)), Leafolio (breeding records) ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/cl/app/leafolio/id6764116802)) and FloraVault (a PDF provenance "health passport") ([mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/floravault/6757129504)). The closest conceptual rival is **Pothy**. It is free and pre-launch, links cuttings to mother plants, and offers a plant-for-plant **swap** in which received cuttings keep their family tree ([Pothy](https://pothy.garden/en/plant-cuttings-app/)). **Rootling: Plant Propagation** (iOS) focuses on photo timelines and reminders rather than lineage ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/rootling-plant-propagation/id6758851487)). None of these shows verified scale. Collectors otherwise use Notion and Gumroad templates at about $5–10 ([Gumroad](https://greenthumbguide.gumroad.com/l/ultimate-plant-care-os-notion)).

Harvest logging is underserved in the edible segment. GrowVeg/Almanac ($35–50/yr), Planter ($24.99/yr or $99.99 lifetime) and Gardenize lead on layout and calendars but have weak records ([Leaftide review](https://leaftide.com/learn/best-garden-planning-apps/); [planter.garden](https://planter.garden/)). Per-pick logging with season summaries shows up mainly in small new apps such as GrowPlanner ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=io.smartpenguins.GrowPlanner&hl=en)) and Garden.gg ([Garden.gg](https://garden.gg/)). **Few apps straddle houseplants and edibles**; From Seed to Spoon, Leaftide and Growli are the main attempts. The flood of 2025–26 indie entrants, promoted through SEO "alternatives" blogs, suggests the hard problem is **distribution, not features**.

## Gamification works when it celebrates intact progress and rewards judgement, not action

The best peer-reviewed streak evidence comes from Silverman and Barasch, *Journal of Consumer Research*, 2023, across seven studies. **Highlighting intact streaks increased later engagement, while highlighting broken ones suppressed it.** The penalty grew when people blamed themselves and shrank when they could repair the streak ([CU Boulder](https://www.colorado.edu/business/faculty-research/2023/04/19/or-track-how-broken-streaks-affect-consumer-decisions)). Barasch's summary was blunt: "people will just drop off once their streaks are broken" ([CU Boulder news](https://www.colorado.edu/business/news/2023/04/20/research-streaks-marketing-tech-barasch)). Duolingo's company-reported data agrees on direction. Learners who reach a 7-day streak are 3.6x likelier to finish a course, and streak freezes lifted DAU by 0.38% ([Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit)). Separating the daily goal from the streak lifted D14 retention by 3.3% ([Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak)), and letting users choose their goal built ownership ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/behind-the-product-duolingo-streaks)). In June 2026 Duolingo let lapsed users earn back 30+ day streaks for free after "tens of thousands" of requests ([Android Authority](https://androidauthority.com/duolingo-revive-broken-streak-event-3673004/)). **Paid streak repair is catalogued as a dark pattern** ([Deceptive.design](https://www.deceptive.design/articles/when-a-user-uses-duolingo-on-a-series-of-consecutive-days-this-is-called-a-streak-if-users-miss-a-day-they-can-pay-to-repair-it)). Duolingo is a high-frequency learning app, so its effect sizes probably won't carry over to an adult hobby app. There is **no published data on streaks in real-plant apps**.

The decisive design analogue is Pokémon Sleep. Its developers "intentionally did not design the app around rewarding specific sleep schedules" because doing so might make people sleep less, and they reward "rhythm and quality" instead ([The Pokémon Company](https://corporate.pokemon.co.jp/en/topics/detail/t-9/)). Plant care has the same trap. **Rewarding watering events trains overwatering**, the single most lethal error. Habitica users relabelled tasks to dodge badly aligned penalties ([LMU](https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/77668)), so users will game whatever Rootline rewards. Plant Nanny, with 10M+ installs, offers a precedent for pushing back on over-action: it warns users who drink too much too fast ([MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2019/03/17/plant-nanny2-water-health-app-review/)). The notes found **no app and no study that explicitly rewards restraint**. Rootline would be the experiment, and that cuts both ways: it is novel, and it is unproven.

Faces and moods are a real lever. People attach to cared-for software agents (the "Tamagotchi effect") ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamagotchi_effect)). But a declining pet reads "as an accusation" under stress, according to the FocusBuddy preprint, whose methods are unverified. Its authors recommend quick forgiveness, celebrating care, and an optional emotional layer ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.00412)). Finch's bird is simply happy when you return ([IU Medicine](https://geneticslab.medicine.iu.edu/blogs/md-student-news/wellness-corner-a-digital-pet-is-your-self-care-best-friend)). Forest kills a tree when you fail, and no test shows that this helps ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_(application))). Confirmshaming is a recognised dark pattern ([Mathur et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.07032)). Real plants actually die, so death or crying faces aimed at the user would compound the self-blame that drives drop-off.

On notifications, survey data is directional only. One survey found 37% of users would disable push at 2–5 per week ([Elaine.io](https://www.elaine.io/en/blog/push-notifications-marketing-friend-foe/)), and MIDiA links higher frequency to higher opt-out ([MIDiA](https://www.midiaresearch.com/blog/chart-of-the-week-over-half-of-app-user-enable-push-notifications)). A collector with 30 plants would blow past that threshold with per-plant pushes, so Rootline should **batch everything into one daily "plant round"** and stay silent when nothing is due. Badges show a small positive effect overall in a 2024 meta-analysis, with inconsistent effects on intrinsic motivation ([Springer ETR&D](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s11423-023-10337-7)). Because plant hobbyists are already intrinsically motivated, badges should recognise milestones and skill rather than serve as the engine. Overjustification is a live risk that the notes did not test.

## Data is cheap if Rootline owns its care table and treats ID as a commodity

Pl@ntNet offers **500 free identifications a day, and commercial use is allowed within that cap**. Pro costs about **€5 per 1,000 IDs** at the 200k tier, and free users must show an acknowledgement sentence and logo ([Pl@ntNet pricing](https://my.plantnet.org/pricing); [terms](https://my.plantnet.org/terms_of_use)). These figures come from search snippets because the official pages returned 503, and the Pro-plan doc describes a **€1,000 upfront payment for 200k requests** that conflicts with the "pay as you go" framing ([Pro plan doc](https://my.plantnet.org/doc/getting-started/pro-plan)). Kindwise (plant.id) is the most polished option. It claims 35k+ classes and 93% top-3 accuracy, and plant.health covers 500+ diseases. It costs **€0.05 down to €0.01 per credit**, new accounts get 100 free credits, and packs under 30k credits don't expire ([Kindwise pricing](https://www.kindwise.com/pricing)). Kindwise returns watering data for **only 9.5% of identifications** ([plant.id](https://www.kindwise.com/plant-id)), so it is an ID and taxonomy service, not a care source.

Care data is the weak link. Perenual is the main live commercial care API. Its free tier allows 100 requests a day and covers only species IDs 1–3,000. Full care guides for 10k+ species need **Supreme at $139.99/month**, and Premium at $59.99 still caps care guides at IDs 1–3,000 ([Perenual](https://perenual.com/subscription-api-pricing)). The data has no stated provenance, so spot-check it. Trefle responds (HTTP 200 and 401 on 2026-10-14) but looks unmaintained ([trefle.io](https://trefle.io/)). **OpenFarm shut down on 21 April 2025** ([FarmBot](https://farm.bot/blogs/news/sunsetting-openfarm)), though a community rescue recovered about 340 CC0 crop records ([growingfruit.org](https://growingfruit.org/t/openfarms-crop-data-restored-in-website/81182)). For taxonomy, the **World Flora Online Plant List is CC0** and releases a JSON dump every six months ([WFO](https://wfoplantlist.org/background); [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/7460932/latest)). That makes it the cleanest legal backbone. GBIF and iNaturalist licences and rate limits were **not verified** this session (pages returned 403). iNaturalist's full vision model is **not downloadable** ([iNat forum](https://forum.inaturalist.org/t/where-to-find-inat-vision-model/12341/2)), and a report of small public models comes from a secondary aggregator only ([feedbagel](https://feedbagel.com/post/inaturalist-releases-subset-of-machine-learning-models-for-public-use)).

Toxicity is where a careless build gets into trouble. The ASPCA list is the de facto reference, but **no reuse licence was found**, and the terms page returned 404 ([ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/animal-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/ivy)). Scrapers exist on Apify ([Apify](https://apify.com/jungle_synthesizer/aspca-toxic-non-toxic-plants-scraper/api)), but that proves nothing about legality. The defensible pattern is a per-species toxic/non-toxic flag in Rootline's own words, a link to the ASPCA page, a disclaimer, and counsel review before launch.

## Collector platforms hand Rootline a data model; plant markets hand it a list of things to avoid

The persona collects vinyl, LEGO and sneakers, and every mature collector platform shares the same structure. A **shared catalogue** (release, set, shoe) sits apart from **owned instances** (my copy, with its own condition and notes) and a separate **want list**. Brickset has per-part "I own"/"I want" flags ([Brickset FAQ](https://brickset.com/faq)). Rebrickable's "Find Sets you can build" turns inventory into possibilities ([Eurobricks](https://www.eurobricks.com/forum/forums/topic/59034-rebrickablecom/page/6)). Discogs shows collection value as an **owner-only min/median/max range** ([python3-discogs-client](https://python3-discogs-client.readthedocs.io/en/v2.5/index.html)), and forum users note the median ignores condition and demand ([Pink Fish Media](https://pinkfishmedia.net/forum/threads/whats-your-discogs-collection-value.264274/page-4)). BrickLink's price guide covers only six months and shows unreliable averages for slow movers ([BrickLink forum](https://www.bricklink.com/messageThread.asp?ID=373556)).

For plants, the mapping is direct. The catalogue entry is a taxon or cultivar, and the owned instance is an individual plant. Variants map to cultivar, variegation type, and tissue culture versus cutting versus seed. A small shared vocabulary for propagule type (node cutting, wet stick, rooted cutting, established plant, TC plantlet) plays the role Goldmine grading plays for vinyl ([Nivessa](https://nivessa.com/blog/grading-your-records-what-every-collector-needs-to-know)), though no source was found for the plant terms themselves. **Lineage is a richer form of provenance than Discogs models**: "this plant is a cutting of plant X, bought from vendor Y on date Z." The Rebrickable counterpart is "what can I propagate now?", driven by node counts and care logs.

The rare-plant market shows what to avoid. A 3-leaf *Anthurium luxurians* 'albo marble' sold for **US$26,000** on Palmstreet in 2025 ([Forbes Australia](https://www.forbes.com.au/life/lifestyle/houseplant-sells-for-26000-on-palmstreet-app-breaking-recent-record/)). Monstera 'Thai Constellation' fell from about $300–1,100 in Australia around 2019 to $99 at Bunnings as tissue culture scaled ([The New Daily](https://www.thenewdaily.com.au/news/2019/08/25/hipster-plant-monstera/); [BHG Australia](https://www.bhg.com.au/rare-collectable-houseplant)). Any valuation feature would mislead quickly. Shipping deaths and refund disputes are the dominant complaints in plant marketplaces ([Justuseapp: Palmstreet](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1514039642/palmstreet-shop-sell-live/reviews)), and Chemistry World notes that variety demand "opened the door to deception and fraud" ([Chemistry World](https://chemistryworld.com/features/the-plant-trades-scientific-secrets/4016068.article)). Swap communities already rely on trust patterns Rootline can reuse later: **timestamped verification photos with username and date, do-not-trade lists, and two-party trade confirmation** ([r/TakeaPlantLeaveaPlant](https://red.applefritter.com/r/TakeaPlantLeaveaPlant/wiki/rules); [r/SteelbookSwap](https://lr.eu.psf.lt/r/SteelbookSwap/wiki/rules/trades)). Sneaker portfolio apps with value leaderboards ([Deal Reaper](https://apps.apple.com/kr/app/id6747319801)) are the cautionary end of that spectrum, because they reward speculation, not care.

The persona's other interests point to inferred, unsourced design hooks. Video games suggest a Pokédex-style journal of species, milestones and "moods seen", which rewards outcomes. Music suggests a "Year in Rootline" recap. Road trips suggest an away mode that hands care to a plant-sitter. None of these is evidenced in the notes; they are hypotheses to test with users.

## Care-reminder logic recommendation

Rootline should schedule **checks, not waterings**. The engine predicts when a plant will probably be dry, prompts a soil check, and learns from the answer. As a formula: `next_check = base_interval(group) × M_light × M_pot_material × M_pot_size × M_mix × M_season_location × k_plant`. Base intervals come from three species groups: succulent/cactus, aroid/tropical foliage, and moisture-lover (fern, calathea). The folk ranges of 7–10 days in summer for tropical foliage and 2–3 weeks for succulents ([houseplant.co.uk](https://www.houseplant.co.uk/blogs/indoor-plant-care/how-often-should-i-water-my-indoor-plants)) are a reasonable seed but are **not evidence**. The directions of the multipliers are well supported: more light, terracotta, smaller pots, chunkier mix and drier air all shorten the interval, while cool, dark months lengthen it ([Illinois Extension](https://extension.illinois.edu/houseplants/watering); [RHS](https://www.rhs.org.uk/garden-inspiration/houseplants/winter-interest-houseplants-care)). The **magnitudes are not established anywhere in the literature** and must start as conservative placeholders.

The per-plant coefficient `k_plant` updates on every check. "Still moist" lengthens it. "Bone dry and wilting" shortens it. A smoothed update such as an exponentially weighted moving average stops one odd day from swinging the schedule. As Greg reportedly does, the model should **err long**, because overwatering is the deadlier mistake. The season multiplier should be keyed to location. Temperate Northern Hemisphere users get longer intervals and paused feeding from about October to February, plus heater and dry-air flags. Accra users get shorter intervals and spider-mite alerts during the harmattan, then longer intervals with gnat and rot alerts during the rains. Air-conditioned rooms are modelled as cool, dry microclimates.

Several guardrails sit on top of that. **Wilting never triggers "water now"**; it triggers "check the soil first", and wilting in wet soil points to possible root rot. A gnat report lengthens the interval and suggests letting the top layer dry. Logging a watering soon after the last one prompts a kind "are you sure? I'm still a bit damp" ([MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2019/03/17/plant-nanny2-water-health-app-review/)). Brown leaf edges trigger a question about recent feeding. Repotting resets the learned coefficient toward the prior and asks the user to re-learn pot weight.

Edibles run on a different engine. In heat, containers move to daily checks with an explicit "keep moisture even" goal to prevent blossom-end rot and splitting. Logging first fruit swell triggers high-potassium feeding every 10–14 days. Forecast thresholds fire a flower-drop risk alert when nights exceed about 24°C or days exceed about 32–35°C, and a hold-transplant alert when temperate nights are below about 10–16°C ([UMD](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/poor-blossom-and-fruit-set-vegetables); [RHS](https://www.rhs.org.uk/vegetables/tomatoes/grow-your-own); [UMN](https://extension.umn.edu/vegetables/growing-peppers)). When the first flower is logged on an indoor or balcony tomato, Rootline schedules a "shake or buzz the trusses every 2–3 days at midday" prompt ([Gardener's Path](https://gardenerspath.com/plants/vegetables/hand-pollinate-tomatoes/), a blog). Scallions get a cut-date log, a regrowth counter, and a "replace after ~3–5 cuts" nudge, which is a blog-sourced range.

Propagation gets its own sequence. Water cuttings get a weekly water change (folk advice), a root check at about week 3, and a pot-up prompt at 2.5–5 cm of root, followed by evenly moist soil for a couple of weeks. All reminders feed the single daily digest.

## Gamification rules

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Make the **soil check** the rewarded unit; give a correct skip equal or greater credit than a watering | Award points, XP or streak credit per watering event |
| Define the streak as "weeks with no overdue checks" or check-ins at a user-chosen cadence ([Lenny's](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/behind-the-product-duolingo-streaks)) | Use a daily "watered" streak, which trains overwatering |
| Feature intact streaks; quietly offer **free** freezes and repair ([JCR 2023](https://www.colorado.edu/business/faculty-research/2023/04/19/or-track-how-broken-streaks-affect-consumer-decisions)) | Spotlight broken streaks or sell streak repair ([Deceptive.design](https://www.deceptive.design/articles/when-a-user-uses-duolingo-on-a-series-of-consecutive-days-this-is-called-a-streak-if-users-miss-a-day-they-can-pay-to-repair-it)) |
| Give faces states like "thirsty but hopeful" and "content/snoozing" after a correct skip, plus a joyful return state (Finch) | Show crying, dying or accusing faces, or "you forgot me again" copy ([Mathur et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.07032)) |
| Reward outcomes: new leaf, first root, first bloom, first harvest, "survived a winter/harmattan", descendants in the family tree | Rank users by care volume, plant count or collection value |
| Write first-person, curious pushes ("Got a sec to check my soil?") batched into one daily round | Send per-plant pushes or nag when nothing is due |
| Offer badges such as "Knows When to Wait" for N correct skips, plus genus checklists and a Pokédex-style journal | Make badges the main engine for already-motivated hobbyists (overjustification risk) |
| Provide a setting to tone down the emotional layer ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.00412)) | Use shared or pairwise social streaks, which create Snapchat-style obligation ([EDRi](https://edri.org/our-work/new-study-reveals-how-young-people-are-influenced-by-gamification-features-on-snapchat/)) |
| Treat a plant death gently: offer to "save a cutting" or record a memorial, keeping the lineage intact | Use Forest-style death as a penalty mechanic |

One honest gap remains. Users can tap "checked" without checking, and the notes contain **no data on how honestly check buttons get used**. Rootline can soften this by asking for a one-tap texture answer (dry / damp / soggy) rather than a bare "done", and by cross-checking against outcomes over time.

## Open questions

Several questions remain, and the notes cannot answer them. On naming: is "Rootline" registrable as a trademark in the US, EU, UK and Ghana, given "Rootling" already exists, and does Rootling's developer hold a mark? On check honesty: do users actually perform the soil check when they tap it, and does a check-based loop measurably improve plant survival over adaptive schedules? No study exists, which makes this Rootline's first experiment. On calibration: what multiplier magnitudes for pot material, pot size, light and harmattan versus heated winter fit real check-outcome data? On Accra: what are actual indoor humidity levels during harmattan, and what sowing windows avoid tomato flower drop in Accra containers? No MoFA/CSIR calendar was found.

The commercial questions are just as open. What pricing do lineage-focused collectors accept, given that the indies are mostly free and Notion templates cost $5–10? Would a lifetime tier (Planter $99.99, Leaftide £99) suit this audience better than an annual plan? How many photo IDs per user per month will a check-first app actually generate? Is Planta's propagation tracking parent-linked, and when does Pothy launch? Will casual plant owners enjoy completion mechanics, or will serious collectors find them childish? The notes found no user research on this. On legal: do Pl@ntNet's full terms allow caching ID results, and what exact attribution do they require? What do current USPTO and UPOV rules say about home propagation and gifting of patented houseplant cultivars, and which popular cultivars are covered? Does "gift a cutting" create liability when the cultivar is patented? Finally, persona fit is untested: do the inferred hooks (away mode for road trips, a Pokédex journal, a year-in-review recap, a propagule vocabulary modelled on vinyl grading) resonate with real users beyond persona zero?

## Conclusion

The research reframes Rootline's playful mechanics as a *safety feature*. In most habit apps, gamification is decoration on the behaviour. In plant care, the obvious gamified behaviour of watering more often is the leading cause of death, so the app that rewards the check and the patient skip is not only kinder but more correct than competitors charging $30–48 a year for schedules users say overwater. The family tree does double duty as the emotional hook (plants as lineages, gifts that keep their history) and as a data asset. Every logged cutting and soil check builds the first real dataset on propagation success and watering intervals across climates from Accra to a heated European flat, an area where the notes repeatedly found no published evidence.

Rootline should launch narrow and opinionated: individual plants, check-based care, location-aware seasons, a lineage tree, edible stage and harvest tracking, and faces that never accuse. It should deliberately refuse marketplaces and valuations, the features that made Discogs and BrickLink build heavy trust infrastructure and that rare-plant markets show to be volatile and fraud-prone. The name needs resolving before anything ships, and the tropical care model should be treated as a hypothesis to calibrate in a local beta, not a finished truth.
